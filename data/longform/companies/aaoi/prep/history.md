# Applied Optoelectronics — corporate history

## Founding and early years (1997-2006)

Applied Optoelectronics, Inc. was incorporated in February 1997 in Sugar Land, Texas by Dr. Chih-Hsiang "Thompson" Lin. Lin was at the time a Senior Research Scientist at the University of Houston (1994-1998), having received his M.S. and Ph.D. in Electrical and Computer Engineering from the University of Missouri-Columbia after an undergraduate degree in Nuclear Engineering from National Tsing Hua University in Taiwan. The company was spun out of his laser-research work and built its first business around laser diode components.

From inception the company has been led by Lin — he has held the combined Chairman / President / CEO role continuously for 28+ years, which is unusually long tenure in a US-listed semiconductor company. Headquarters has remained in Sugar Land, Texas since 2000.

## Vertical integration build-out (2006-2014)

Through the late 2000s AAOI built a vertically integrated stack that became its structural differentiation: in-house InP and GaAs laser-diode wafer fabrication using molecular beam epitaxy (MBE) and metal-organic chemical vapour deposition (MOCVD), plus subsystem and transceiver assembly. The company added Taiwan and China manufacturing footprints to complement the Texas headquarters — Taipei (Taiwan) for wafer fab and module assembly, and Ningbo (China) for laser-device fabrication and transceiver assembly at scale.

The company's products through this period split between (a) cable broadband / HFC networking equipment for the CATV industry, (b) FTTH lasers and transceivers for the telecom market, and (c) early-generation datacenter optical transceivers.

## IPO and the first datacenter cycle (2013-2017)

AAOI completed its NASDAQ IPO in September 2013. The first major cyclical lift came with the 100G datacenter transceiver ramp at Microsoft and Amazon between 2015 and 2017, during which revenue accelerated to a peak of $382 million in FY2017 and the stock — which had IPO'd around $10 — touched approximately $96 in mid-2017. This was AAOI's first encounter with the dynamic that defines its operating model: a single hyperscaler product-generation cycle can multiply revenue or halve it.

## The trough (2018-2023)

From 2018 onwards AAOI entered a multi-year operational trough. Microsoft's hyperscale buying pattern shifted to other suppliers; Amazon de-emphasised AAOI as a primary; the CATV cycle weakened as cable MSOs deferred upgrade capex; and the company's 400G ramp was repeatedly delayed by qualification slips. Revenue declined from $382M (2017) to a low of $187M (2023, approximate). Accumulated deficit grew sharply as gross margins compressed under fixed-cost pressure, ultimately reaching $493.1M by year-end 2025.

This was the period during which the stock collapsed from $96 to a 2023 low near $1.50, a decline of more than 98%. The company carried out multiple equity raises through this trough and incurred substantial impairment and inventory write-downs.

## The dual-cycle recovery (2024-2025)

Two simultaneous cycles turned in 2024-2025. First, the cable industry returned to capex spending on DOCSIS 4.0 / 1.8 GHz amplifier upgrades, driving the CATV business through Digicomm to a record level — Digicomm's share of AAOI's revenue rose from 11.3% (2023) to 34.1% (2024) to 53.1% (2025). Second, the AI buildout drove hyperscalers — particularly Microsoft and Amazon — into urgent 800G optical transceiver buying. AAOI's vertically integrated stack and its in-region Texas/Taiwan/China manufacturing footprint became an advantage rather than overhead.

FY2025 revenue reached $455.7 million (+82.8% YoY), GAAP gross margin recovered to 30.0% (from 24.8%), and net loss narrowed to $38.2M from $186.7M. Q4 2025 was the first quarter in company history that datacenter revenue ($74.9M) exceeded CATV revenue ($54.0M) — the mix is in the process of flipping back toward the AI infrastructure end-market.

## The Amazon warrant (March 2025) and the 1.6T order (March 2026)

In March 2025, AAOI granted Amazon a stock purchase warrant for up to 7.945 million shares as part of a hyperscale supply relationship. The warrant vests against purchase volume thresholds. Through 2025 Amazon was not yet a >10% customer in the 10-K disclosure — the recognition lag reflects purchase-cycle timing rather than relationship absence.

Between March and April 2026, AAOI announced a sequence of order announcements from "a major hyperscale customer" totalling over $324 million: $53M (800G) and >$200M (1.6T first-volume) in March, plus an additional $71M (800G) in early April. The 1.6T order is significant because it positions AAOI as one of a small number of optical transceiver suppliers with qualified product at the line rate above 800G — a generation transition that the broader market expects to dominate AI datacenter optics through 2027.

## Where the story sits at snapshot (May 23, 2026)

AAOI is a 28-year-old founder-led optical components and transceivers maker that has, in the past 24 months, executed a structural recovery from existential balance-sheet damage. The stock has moved from $1.50 to approximately $181 over five years, a roughly 120x move that compresses three things: (a) the CATV cyclical recovery, (b) the AI-optics ramp, and (c) the option value of being one of a small handful of vertically-integrated optical transceiver suppliers qualified at 800G and starting on 1.6T. The accumulated deficit of $493.1M and the 81.9% customer concentration (Digicomm + Microsoft) remain the structural risks. The Amazon warrant relationship and the >$324M near-term order book are the open-ended upside.
