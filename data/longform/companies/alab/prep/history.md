# Astera Labs — corporate history

## Founding (November 2017)

Astera Labs was founded in November 2017 in Santa Clara, California by three Texas Instruments alumni — Jitendra Mohan (CEO), Sanjay Gajendra (President/COO), and Casey Morrison (Chief Product Officer). Mohan and Gajendra had both spent the prior decade at Texas Instruments after careers at National Semiconductor (acquired by TI in 2011); the three founders left TI to start the company on the thesis that PCIe and CXL connectivity silicon would become the rate-limiting layer of next-generation server architectures.

The Series A was led by Sutter Hill Ventures (partner Stefan Dyckerhoff joined the board in 2019), with subsequent rounds from Fidelity, GlobalFoundries Ventures, Intel Capital, and Avigdor Willenz Group (Willenz is the founder of Galileo Technology and Annapurna Labs and the lead independent figure behind Manuel Alba's later board appointment).

## Pre-IPO build-out (2018-2023)

Through 2018-2023 Astera built out its four product families — Aries (PCIe/CXL retimers), Taurus (Ethernet smart cable modules), Leo (CXL memory controllers), and Scorpio (fabric switches) — and secured design-ins at hyperscalers and AI-server OEMs. The company headquartered in Santa Clara, with engineering centres in Israel (Astera Labs Israel, run by SVP Guy Azrad) and India (Bangalore, run by SVP Shivananda R. Koteshwar).

The board added Manuel Alba as Chairman in March 2018; Stefan Dyckerhoff (Sutter Hill) in 2019; Michael Hurlston in 2022; Jack Lazar in December 2022; Bethany Mayer in June 2024; and Craig Barratt in March 2025. The composition was assembled methodically, with each addition layering a specific kind of independent governance.

## IPO (March 2024)

Astera Labs completed its NASDAQ IPO in March 2024. The offering priced at $36 per share (above the initial $32-34 range) and opened for trading on March 20, 2024 at $52.56 — a 71% first-day jump that valued the company at approximately $9.5 billion at the IPO and over $13 billion at the close.

The IPO came at the precise moment AI infrastructure demand was accelerating: the company had reported FY2023 revenue of $115.8 million (+45% YoY), but the FY2024 revenue trajectory (which ended at $396.3 million, +242% from FY23) was already visible in the Q1 2024 print published shortly after the IPO.

## First full year as a public company (FY2024)

FY2024 produced $396.3 million of revenue (+242% YoY from $115.8M FY2023) on GAAP gross margin in the low-70%s. The company became GAAP-profitable during 2024 — a relatively unusual outcome for a Series-funded silicon company in its first public year, attributable to (a) the high-margin design-in nature of connectivity-silicon ASICs and (b) the operating leverage produced by the 242% revenue growth landing against a fixed engineering cost base built for >$500M of revenue scale.

## The structural-margin year (FY2025)

FY2025 (the year being reported in the 10-K filed February 26, 2026) produced:
- Revenue $852.5M (+115% YoY)
- GAAP gross margin 75.7% (up from low-70%s prior)
- GAAP net income $219.1M ($1.22 diluted EPS)
- Non-GAAP net income $331.0M ($1.84 diluted)
- Operating cash flow $319.3M
- Q4 2025 standalone: $270.6M revenue (+92% YoY, +17% sequential)

Cash and marketable securities at December 31, 2025: $1.19 billion against essentially no debt.

Quarter-by-quarter sequential growth was the operational story of the year: Q3 2025 (announced October 28, 2025) was Astera's first $200M+ revenue quarter, and Q4 2025 (announced February 24, 2026) was the first $250M+ quarter. The Q1 2026 guidance of $286-297 million suggested continued mid-single-digit sequential growth into the next architecture transition.

## Board additions through 2025-2026

Two changes reshaped the senior leadership in 2025-2026:

**Craig Barratt joined the board in March 2025.** A Stanford EE PhD with a CEO/President resume at Atheros, Qualcomm Atheros, Barefoot Networks, and Intel Connectivity Group, Barratt was simultaneously serving on the Intel board (joined November 2025) where he was designated incoming Chairman effective May 13, 2026. The dual appointment to one of the most consequential US silicon companies and to Astera at the same time was widely interpreted as a signal of how seriously the AI-connectivity silicon stack would matter to Intel's recovery.

**Desmond Lynch became CFO effective March 2026**, replacing Mike Tate (who retired March 2, 2026 with transition services through September 1, 2026). Lynch had been SVP Finance and CFO at Rambus 2022-2026 — the appointment signals continued focus on IP-and-royalty-style economics within the Astera product mix.

## Where the story sits at snapshot (May 23, 2026)

Astera Labs is two-and-a-half years into life as a public company. It has produced two consecutive years of triple-digit revenue growth (242% in FY24, 115% in FY25), structural gross-margin discipline (75.7% GAAP in FY25), GAAP profitability, $1.19B of liquid balance sheet, and a board that has been assembled deliberately for ongoing cycle-compression governance rather than for one-time IPO execution. The strategic question for the next 18-24 months is whether Astera retains design-in position at the Nvidia Rubin / AMD MI400-series generation and successfully scales Scorpio fabric silicon into the volume tier — both of which determine whether the company sustains the >$1B annual revenue trajectory implied by the FY26 guidance.
