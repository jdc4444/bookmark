# Dell Technologies — corporate history

## Founding (1984)

Michael Dell founded Dell Computer Corporation in 1984 in his University of Texas at Austin dorm room at age 19. The original business model was direct-to-customer PC sales, bypassing the retail distribution channel that dominated the PC market in the 1980s. The company was incorporated as PCs Limited in May 1984, renamed Dell Computer Corporation in 1987, and went public on NASDAQ in June 1988.

## The 1990s consumer-PC dominance

Through the 1990s Dell became the dominant US PC manufacturer, executing on direct-to-customer + just-in-time manufacturing + lean inventory. By 2001 Dell was the #1 PC vendor globally. Michael Dell stepped down as CEO in 2004 (remaining Chairman) and Kevin Rollins served as CEO 2004-2007. Dell returned as CEO in January 2007 amid declining margins and market share.

## The 2013 take-private LBO ($24.4B)

In October 2013 Michael Dell and Silver Lake Partners completed a $24.4 billion leveraged buyout of Dell Computer Corporation, taking the company private. Egon Durban joined the board concurrent with the transaction representing Silver Lake. The take-private was motivated by Michael Dell's view that Dell needed to invest in enterprise capabilities — particularly in data center, storage, and services — beyond what public-market quarterly pressures would permit.

## The 2016 EMC acquisition ($67B)

In September 2016 Dell completed the acquisition of EMC Corporation in a $67 billion transaction — at the time the largest technology M&A deal in history. The combined company was rebranded Dell Technologies. The EMC acquisition brought in:
- EMC's enterprise storage portfolio (VNX, VMAX, XtremIO, Isilon, Data Domain)
- VMware (held as a separately-traded tracking stock until 2021 spin-off)
- Pivotal Software (later folded back into VMware)
- RSA Security (later divested)
- VirtuStream cloud services
- SecureWorks (still partially-held)

The combined entity became the largest US privately-held technology company.

## The 2018 relisting via VMware tracking stock exchange

In December 2018 Dell Technologies returned to the public markets through a complex transaction in which Dell exchanged its VMware Class V tracking stock (DVMT) for newly-issued Dell Class C common stock, listing on NYSE under the ticker DELL.

## The 2021 VMware spin-off

In November 2021 Dell spun off VMware as a separately-traded company; VMware was subsequently acquired by Broadcom in 2023 in a $69B transaction.

## The 2018-2023 enterprise-AI build-out

Through 2018-2023 Dell built out the Infrastructure Solutions Group (ISG) capability around AI-optimised servers, storage, networking, and the Dell AI Factory initiative. The PowerEdge server line was expanded with Nvidia H100 and AMD MI300X-equipped configurations through 2023.

## The FY2025 AI server inflection

Fiscal year 2025 (calendar year roughly 2024) was the year Dell's AI server business reached scale: AI server orders accelerated dramatically through the year as xAI, CoreWeave, and broader hyperscaler buyers placed multi-billion-dollar orders. The AI Factory initiative formalised the integrated AI infrastructure stack.

## The FY2026 record year

Fiscal year 2026 (ended approximately January 30, 2026) was the record AI server year:
- Total revenue $113.5B
- AI server revenue $24.6B (+300%+ YoY)
- AI server orders closed during the year: $64B
- AI server shipments: $25B+
- Year-end AI server backlog: $43B
- Q4 FY26 ISG revenue: $18.82B (+66% YoY); server and networking +112%
- Q4 FY26 CSG revenue: $13.5B (+14%); commercial $11.6B (+16%), consumer $1.9B (flat)

## The September 2025 CFO transition

On September 8, 2025 Dell announced that CFO Yvonne McGill would transition from the CFO role effective September 9, 2025, after nearly 30 years with Dell. David Kennedy (SVP Dell Global Business Operations and Finance, 27 years at Dell, prior CSG CFO and Global Sales COO) was appointed interim CFO. McGill remains in advisory capacity through Q3 fiscal 2026. A comprehensive search for a permanent CFO is underway.

## The 2026 redomestication to Texas

The board has unanimously recommended Dell Technologies' redomestication from Delaware to Texas, consistent with the broader trend of large US companies moving from Delaware to Texas through 2025-2026. The transaction reflects Dell's Texas operating headquarters (Round Rock, TX) being the appropriate corporate domicile rather than Delaware corporate formation.

## Where the story sits at snapshot (May 23, 2026)

Dell Technologies is 42 years into Michael Dell's founder-CEO franchise, operating at $113.5B FY26 revenue with $43B AI server backlog entering FY27 and the most operationally complex US technology integration footprint outside the hyperscalers themselves. The investment proposition is whether the AI server cycle sustains long enough for Dell's $43B backlog to compound through FY27-28 into a durable operating-margin uplift, and whether the CSG AI PC refresh cycle adds a second growth engine to the consolidated story. The September 2025 CFO transition and the Texas redomestication are the contemporary governance events shaping the next phase.
