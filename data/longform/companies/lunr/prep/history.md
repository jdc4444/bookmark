# Intuitive Machines — corporate history

## Founding (2013)

Intuitive Machines was co-founded in 2013 in Houston by Stephen Altemus and Dr. Kamal Ghaffarian. Altemus had spent his career at NASA — starting at Kennedy Space Center in 1989, moving to Johnson Space Center where he served as a lead engineer in the Engineering Directorate's Aeroscience and Flight Mechanics Division and led the Columbia Reconstruction Director role after the loss of the Space Shuttle Columbia on February 1, 2003 — and ultimately served as Deputy Director of JSC. Ghaffarian was the co-founder of Stinger Ghaffarian Technologies (eventually acquired by KBR in 2018) and a serial founder of deep-tech companies including Axiom Space, X Energy, Quantum Space, and PTX.

The original thesis was that NASA's exploration mandate would shift from agency-owned execution to commercial procurement — and that a Houston-based aerospace company with NASA institutional credibility could be the private partner for lunar logistics, deep space, and adjacent capabilities.

## CLPS award (2018) and lander development (2019-2023)

In 2018 NASA selected Intuitive Machines as one of the initial Commercial Lunar Payload Services (CLPS) program participants, providing the institutional procurement channel for the company's lunar lander development. Through 2019-2023 the company built out its Nova-C lander platform — a class designed to deliver up to 100 kg of NASA and commercial payloads to the lunar surface.

## De-SPAC and public listing (February 2023)

In February 2023 Intuitive Machines completed its public listing via business combination with Inflection Point Acquisition Corp. — a SPAC sponsored by Michael Blitzer's Inflection Point organization. Blitzer joined the board concurrent with the de-SPAC.

The company began trading on NASDAQ under the ticker LUNR.

## Board build-out (February 2023 - June 2023)

Concurrent with the de-SPAC, the board was formed with:
- Class III: Dr. Kamal Ghaffarian (Chairman), Stephen Altemus (CEO)
- Class II: Michael Blitzer (Kingstown Capital founder, Inflection Point SPAC sponsor)
- Class I: William J. Liquori (retired Lt. General US Space Force), Robert L. Masson (ex-Raytheon CFO, US Naval Academy)

In June 2023 the company appointed Nicole Seligman (ex-President Sony Entertainment, ex-Global GC Sony Corporation, Harvard Law magna, OpenAI director) to the board as a Class II director.

## IM-1 'Odysseus' lunar landing (February 22, 2024)

On February 15, 2024 Intuitive Machines launched the Nova-C IM-1 mission 'Odysseus' aboard a SpaceX Falcon 9. The lander touched down on the Moon's surface near the Malapert A crater on February 22, 2024 — becoming the first privately-built spacecraft to successfully land on the Moon and the first US-built spacecraft to soft-land on the Moon since Apollo 17 in 1972.

The landing had operational complications: a landing-leg failure caused the lander to come to rest on its side, partially obscuring solar panels and limiting mission duration. Despite this, IM-1 transmitted data and demonstrated commercial lunar landing as a viable capability.

## IM-2 'Athena' (Q1 2025)

The IM-2 mission ('Athena') launched in early 2025 carrying additional NASA and commercial payloads. The lander reached the lunar surface but mission outcome had similar operational complications. IM-2 demonstrated that the platform could be replicated at the lander class but also highlighted the operational difficulty of lunar surface operations at the precision required.

## CFO transition (September 2024) and executive build-out

Peter McGrath joined as CFO in September 2024 from a 31-year Boeing career across structural analysis, Phantom Works, and launch services. McGrath had previously been Intuitive Machines' VP of Business Development. The CFO transition formalised the financial-leadership function as the company moved from de-SPAC into operating-scale.

## NASA CLPS contract ceiling expansion (2024-2025) and IM-5 task order

Through 2024-2025 NASA expanded the CLPS contract ceiling to approximately $4.82 billion, with $150 million guaranteed near-term across task orders. The IM-5 task order (announced for the larger cargo-class Nova-D lander, $180.4 million in value) was the largest single CLPS task order awarded to date — directing seven science and technology payloads to the Lunar South Pole.

## Lanteris Space Systems acquisition (announced 2025-2026)

The single most consequential transaction in Intuitive Machines' history was the announcement of the $800 million acquisition of Lanteris Space Systems — $450 million cash plus $350 million stock, expected to close Q1 2026. Chris Johnson (CEO of Lanteris, ex-President of Boeing Satellite Systems International, 20+ years at Boeing) was named President of Lanteris Space Systems within Intuitive Machines effective January 2026.

The acquisition explicitly transforms Intuitive Machines from a pure-play lunar delivery specialist into a full space prime contractor with satellite-systems product lines. Total backlog post-Lanteris rises to $943 million.

## Executive realignment (February 2026)

Effective February 2026:
- Dr. Timothy Crain (co-founder, ex-NASA JSC engineer on Mars Science Lander and Orion spacecraft GNC) became Senior Vice President and Chief Technology Officer
- Anna Jones (ex-Paysafe VP Securities and Corporate Counsel, ex-Marathon Oil Assistant GC, ex-Latham and Watkins, JD Northwestern Law) became SVP, Chief Legal Officer, and Corporate Secretary

## FY2025 financial results

For FY2025 Intuitive Machines reported:
- Q4 revenue $44.8M (-18% YoY, reflecting the lumpy NASA milestone-payment recognition pattern)
- Q4 gross margin 19%
- Full-year revenue in the $250-300M range

## FY2026 outlook

Management has guided FY2026 revenue of $900M-$1B (~3-4x FY25) with positive Adjusted EBITDA — implying the operating-profitability inflection that defines the next phase of the company's life.

## Where the story sits at snapshot (May 23, 2026)

Intuitive Machines has completed its de-SPAC, executed two lunar landings (IM-1 and IM-2 both reaching the Moon's surface with operational complications but functional missions), expanded its NASA CLPS contract ceiling to $4.82 billion, announced the transformational $800M Lanteris acquisition, and guided FY2026 revenue of $900M-$1B with positive Adjusted EBITDA. The investment proposition is whether the Lanteris integration converts the lunar-logistics specialist into a sustainable space prime contractor over the FY2026-2028 horizon, and whether the NASA Artemis program sustains the lunar-economy thesis that justifies the company's positioning.
