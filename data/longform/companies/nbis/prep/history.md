# Nebius Group — corporate history

## The Yandex origin (1990-2024)

Nebius Group N.V. is the Netherlands-domiciled successor entity to Yandex N.V. — the holding company that owned Yandex, Russia's dominant internet search and technology company. The earliest progenitor was CompTek International, co-founded in 1990 by John Boynton (who has remained on the board since 2000 and as chairman since 2016) and which incubated Yandex itself. Yandex's NASDAQ IPO occurred in 2011 and the company built out the dominant Russian search-engine business plus ride-hailing, food delivery, e-commerce, and adjacent products through the 2010s.

Arkady Volozh — Yandex's co-founder and CEO from 2000 to 2022 — stepped down from the chief-executive role in 2022 in the context of post-invasion political pressure that affected the public listing and Western shareholder base. Volozh remained at the Yandex board and ownership level through the subsequent restructuring.

## The 2024 restructuring

In 2024, Yandex N.V. completed a major corporate restructuring that separated the Russian operating business (which was sold to a consortium of Russian investors) from the Netherlands-domiciled NASDAQ-listed parent. The post-restructuring parent retained the international assets and was rebranded Nebius Group N.V., trading on NASDAQ under the ticker NBIS.

The retained international portfolio comprised:
- The core AI cloud business — Nvidia GPU clusters in European data centers, repositioned as a full-stack AI infrastructure offering
- Toloka AI — global human-data labelling platform
- TripleTen — online tech education
- Avride — autonomous driving technology

Volozh resumed the CEO role in 2024 to lead the strategic refocus on AI infrastructure. The board was reconfigured with a mix of Yandex-legacy figures (Boynton chairman, Charles Ryan independent) and newer arrivals calibrated for AI infrastructure governance.

## The 2024-2025 board build-out

Through 2024-2025 the board added:
- **Ophir Nave (May 2024 as COO, August 2024 as Executive Director)** — Harvard SJD, Wachtell Lipton, Tel Aviv University corporate-finance lecturer. Nave ran the post-restructuring legal-architecture work.
- **Elena Bunina (August 2024 as Non-Executive Director)** — ex-Yandex HR director and academic services head, now leads Nebius Science and Education and the TripleTen-adjacent function.
- **Kira Radinsky (2024 as Independent Non-Executive Director)** — Diagnostic Robotics CEO, AI-healthcare specialist, ex-SalesPredict (sold to eBay 2016).

At the August 2025 AGM:
- **Arne Grimme** (Independent Non-Executive Director) — De Brauw Blackstone Westbroek partner, Head of Corporate M&A practice; anchors the Netherlands-domicile governance.
- **Matthew Weigand** (Independent Non-Executive Director) — Accel partner; joined concurrent with Accel's participation in the $700M strategic equity placement.

The board at the snapshot date is eight directors: Volozh (CEO), Nave (COO), Bunina, plus five non-executive directors (Boynton chairman, Ryan, Radinsky, Grimme, Weigand).

## The 2025 strategic equity financing

In 2025 Nebius closed a $700M oversubscribed strategic equity placement from a select group of institutional and accredited investors including Accel, Nvidia, and certain Orbis Investments accounts. The Nvidia participation is the most strategically consequential element: Nvidia is simultaneously Nebius's dominant GPU supplier AND a strategic equity investor, creating circular alignment that informs Nvidia GPU allocation priority for the FY2026 capacity build.

## FY2025 operating results: the inflection year

Nebius FY2025 produced (per Q4 / FY2025 release in February 2026):
- Q4 2025 revenue $227.7 million (+547% YoY); core AI cloud segment +802% YoY
- Q4 2025 net loss $249.6 million (depreciation and stock-comp impact of scale-up)
- Q4 2025 operating cash flow $834 million (customer-prepayment signature)
- Q4 2025 capex ~$2 billion (AI processor procurement + data center build-out)
- FY2025 exit ARR $1.25 billion
- FY2025 exit cash $3.7 billion
- FY2025 exit power 170MW active, >2GW contracted
- FY2025 adjusted EBITDA margin (core) 24% in Q4, up from 19% in Q3

The Q4 revenue print of $227.7M was below analyst expectations of approximately $247M — the miss did not change the FY2026 guidance trajectory.

## FY2026 guidance: the most aggressive in the peer set

Management's FY2026 guidance:
- Revenue $3.0-3.4 billion (roughly 7x FY2025)
- Adjusted EBITDA margin ~40% (significantly above the 24% Q4 2025 print)
- Capex $16-20 billion
- Year-end ARR $7-9 billion with more than half already contracted
- Year-end connected power 800MW-1GW (5-6x the year-end-2025 active power)

The guidance compresses three execution challenges: (a) capex deployment of $16-20B against $3.7B starting cash, (b) ARR scaling from $1.25B to $7-9B in twelve months with >50% already contracted, and (c) connected power scaling from 170MW to 800MW-1GW. The Nvidia strategic relationship and the customer-prepayment cash dynamics are the structural enablers; the question is whether Nebius can convert contracted commitments into deployed capacity inside the guidance window.

## Where the story sits at snapshot (May 23, 2026)

Nebius is the most aggressive AI-infrastructure ramp in the public-equity peer set. The Yandex-legacy board provides institutional memory; the post-restructuring additions provide the strategic capability set required to operate as a Netherlands-domiciled NASDAQ-listed AI cloud company; the Accel/Nvidia/Orbis strategic equity placement provides the financing alignment. The investment proposition compresses corporate-restructuring execution, balance-sheet rebuild, and a global-scale AI infrastructure capacity ramp against a guidance bar that few competitors have publicly committed to.
