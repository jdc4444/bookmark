# Unitika — corporate history

Source: company-histories.com + Wikipedia + Japanese sources

## 1889–1918 — Foundation: Amagasaki Spinners

- **1889**: **Amagasaki Spinners Ltd.** established in Hyogo by Osaka merchants and bankers
- **1895**: 27,000+ spindles, among Japan's dozen largest spinning concerns
- **1908–1918**: Aggressive consolidation
  - 1908 → Toyo Textile
  - 1914 → Tokyo Spinning
  - 1916 → Nihon Spinning
  - 1918 → Settu Spinning
- Combined entity becomes **Dainippon Spinning Company**

## 1919–1939 — Innovation under Dainippon

- 1920s financial crisis: hedge-selling raw cotton
- **1926**: Established **Nippon Rayon Company** (synthetic fiber diversification)
- ECO-type high-draft spindle developed (industry standard)
- Adopted Toyota automatic looms 1926+
- 1932 Sekigahara unit-drive plant

## 1940–1955 — War + Recovery

- 1940–41 wartime reorganization → 14 spinning units; Dainippon intact
- 1943 amalgamations: "Big Ten" spinners
- Post-1945 SCAP threatened breakup, reversed
- 1951 production controls lifted
- METI-directed shift toward synthetic/chemical exports
- **1965**: Established **Nippon Ester Co., Ltd.** as JV
- Mid-1950s: 15-year high-growth period begins

## 1964–1969 — The Unitika name is born

- **1964**: Dainippon renamed **Nichibo**
- **1969**: **Nichibo merges with Nippon Rayon Company → Unitika Ltd.**

## 1970–1989 — Crisis + diversification

- 1969–72 US textile negotiations + 1973 oil shock ends prosperity
- 1975: Closed 2 cotton staple mills, cut synthetic fiber output
- 1977: Withdrew from Singapore cotton-spinning JV
- **1978**: First major industry firm to cut workforce — 650 redundancies
- 1979: Commercialized anti-static nylon filament for work garments
- International JVs: Hong Kong, Italy, US
- **1981**: Plan to replace ~1,000 looms with 400 modern
- 1983: R&D consortium with 7 competitors — 17x more productive cotton spinning
- 1986: ~800 employees shed
- 1989: Non-fibers = 33% of sales; **resumed dividends for first time since 1977**

## 1990–1999 — Restructuring decade

- 1991: Women's clothing material JV with Dollfus Meig (France); subsidiaries in Indonesia, Hong Kong, Thailand
- **1994**: Posted losses; exited rayon filament; textiles -14.5%, non-textiles +6.6%
- 1997–98: Sales fell with Japan's economy
- **1999**: Spun off textiles + fibers into subsidiaries **Unitika Textiles Ltd. and Unitika Fibers Ltd.**

## 2000–2002 — Strategic consolidation

- 2000 business plan: halve 92 subsidiaries by 2003
- Five operating units: synthetic fibers, polymers, environment, functional materials, life/health
- 2002: Sales -11%, net income -66% to $10M
- Cancelled planned spun-bond facility
- **2002 snapshot**: 1,345 employees, $1.9B sales

## 2009 — Bio-plastic milestone

- New plastic exceeding ABS in production-carbon, heat, impact durability

## 2014 — First failed restructuring attempt

- Began moving fiber production to Thailand
- Cost cuts — but ultimately unsuccessful, especially with weak yen

## 2024-11-29 — Withdrawal announcement

- Formal exit from textile/fiber announced
- Plan published under REVIC support

## 2025-04-30 — Capital restructuring

- Cancelled Class A + Class B preferred shares
- Issued Class C preferred to REVIC (¥20B, 66.76% voting)
- Banks (MUFG, Mizuho, SMTB) approve up to ¥43B debt forgiveness
- Board reconstituted: 7 outside directors, 1 outside auditor
- Fujii Minoru appointed President & Representative Director

## 2025–2026 — Active divestiture phase

- 2025-08: Target deadline for divestiture agreements
- 2025-10: Nonwovens buyer found
- 2025-11-04: Company split agreement w/ wholly-owned subsidiary executed
- 2025-12: DIC × Unitika joint PPS film for mmWave announced (CES 2026)
- 2026-02-06: Q3 FY3/26 results — 9M cumulative revenue ¥95.6B (+2.2%), OP ¥9.0B (+110.3%)
- Seiren Co. acquiring Okazaki textile business (jointly with Nippon Ester)
- COTEX acquired spunlace nonwovens
- Tusco (Thailand polyester spunbond) still seeking buyer
